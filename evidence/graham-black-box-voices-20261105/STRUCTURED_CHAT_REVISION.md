You ask AI a question. It gives you an answer.

You ask how it got there. It gives you an explanation.

Now you have two things that sound reasonable. How much have you actually checked?

That’s what interests me about the AI “black box.”

Anthropic published research in 2025 tracing parts of what happens inside one of its models. Even on short prompts, the researchers acknowledged that their method captured only a fraction of the computation.

They could see some of the workings. Plenty remained outside that view.

Meanwhile, we can have an ordinary conversation with the thing. Ask follow-up questions. Push back. Get a clearer answer.

I can see how that could make the limits feel further away than they are.

Incomplete understanding doesn’t automatically make something unreliable. I couldn’t explain every system I depend on.

But I’d want my confidence to have something behind it beyond a good conversation.

If I’m exploring an idea, I can leave questions open. If I’m using an answer to make a commitment, I’d want to check the sources, assumptions and consequences more carefully.

The box can stay partly mysterious.

My reason for trusting a particular answer should be a little clearer.
