AI is a strange thing to trust.

You type something into a box. An answer comes back, neatly organized, well written, sometimes surprisingly good.

You can even ask it to explain itself.

That feels reassuring. Although an explanation is another answer from the same box.

Anthropic published research exploring parts of what happens inside one of its models. The researchers could trace some of the activity, while acknowledging how much their methods still couldn’t capture.

I find that fascinating.

We’re figuring out how to use these systems while researchers are still figuring out how to understand them.

And honestly, incomplete understanding is something we already live with. I couldn’t explain every system I rely on.

But with AI, the conversation itself can feel like evidence. The answer makes sense. The follow-up sounds reasonable. It handles an objection.

At what point have I checked the answer, and at what point have I just had a convincing conversation?

That’s the question I keep coming back to.

For a rough idea, I’m comfortable leaving plenty unresolved. For something that could affect a customer, a commitment or someone else’s work, I’d want more to go on.

A source I can open. A claim I can check. Someone with the experience to spot what I might miss.

I don’t need to understand every calculation inside the box to find it useful.

I do want to stay aware of the gap between a convincing answer and a reason to rely on it.
