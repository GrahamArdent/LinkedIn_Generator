Packing a parcel quickly saves little if it arrives unusable.

AI’s first output is the packed box. Checking, correcting and finishing it still take work.

Stanford studied 5,172 customer support agents and found a 15% average increase in issues resolved per hour with AI assistance. Less experienced agents improved in speed and quality. The most experienced saw small speed gains and small quality declines.

METR reported that some developers noticed differences in final code quality, tests and documentation between work with and without AI. METR said its later productivity estimate was unreliable.

Neither study measures how much checking shaped those results. So I would ask: how much work remains after the first output before someone can use it?

That is where I would count the saving.
